1. The short version

Everything the assistant does today — the three-step booking, the document reading, the tracking, the agent hand-off, the Operations console — lives behind one conversation engine that does not care which messaging app the client is using. Telegram is one "transport" plugged into it. WhatsApp is a second transport plugged into the same engine, the same database and the same console. Both can run side by side; the console already shows which channel a request came from.

What carries over unchanged: the three steps, the chassis check, the readers for pasted details and captions, the document pipeline, one reply for several files, the summary card, the booking reference and PDF, the desk's workflow, tracking, the after-hours rule with the urgent question, every safety rule, and all 237 tests.
What WhatsApp adds: a Meta business account and a verified business, a dedicated phone number, a 24-hour window after which the assistant may only send pre-approved template messages, buttons limited to three per message (longer choices become a list), no way to pin a status card, and a per-message price for messages MKY starts. Setting up the accounts is the long pole: verification usually takes two to ten business days.

4. WhatsApp's rules that shape the design

4.1 The 24-hour customer service window

From the moment a client sends a message, the business may reply freely for 24 hours. Every step of a booking, a tracking request, a question — all of that happens inside the window, because the client is the one writing. It is free of charge.

Outside the window the business may only send template messages: texts approved in advance by Meta, with placeholders for the reference, the vessel and so on, in a category (utility, marketing, authentication) that sets the price. The assistant's messages that can fall outside the window are the ones the desk triggers later:

Event todayTemplate on WhatsApp (utility category)
Booking confirmed by Operations, with the PDF and shipment reference"Your booking {1} is confirmed. Shipment {2}. Your PDF copy is attached." — document header
Operations asks for more information"We need a little more to continue your booking {1}: {2}. Reply here to send it."
Booking rejected, with the reason"We were not able to confirm request {1}. Reason: {2}. Reply here to reach the team."
MRN issued"MRN {1} has been issued for your booking {2}."
Shipment update the desk chooses to send (vessel, ETA, arrival)"Update on shipment {1}: {2}."
A message an agent types to the client"A message from our team about {1}: {2}."

The client tapping "Reply" on any of these reopens a 24-hour window, and the conversation continues as normal. The outbox that already exists — every message queued with a key, retried until delivered, never sent twice — decides per message whether it is inside the window (send as-is) or outside (send the matching template).

4.2 Buttons and lists

  • A message may carry at most three reply buttons, each up to 20 characters. Most of the assistant's choices fit: the main menu, the MRN choice, Confirm / Edit / Cancel, Yes urgent / No it can wait, Continue / Start over.
  • Longer choices use a list message: one "Choose" button that opens up to ten rows. The edit menu (seven items) and "which document is this?" (up to six) become lists.
  • Buttons cannot be removed from a message after it is sent, so a stale button on an old message stays visible. The engine already refuses a stale tap and says why — that behaviour matters more on WhatsApp.
  • Typed numbers ("2" for the second choice) keep working as they do on the website.

4.3 Media

PDFs, images and documents arrive with a media id; the assistant fetches the file from Meta with its token within Meta's retention period (about 30 days) and stores it in MKY's own storage, as today. Captions travel with the file and are read as the details of the booking. Several files sent at once arrive as separate messages; the batch handling built for Telegram — record each file, read them in parallel, one reply — applies unchanged.

4.4 Identity

The client's WhatsApp number is their identity; there is no username. It becomes the client record's phone, which is why step 1 is one question shorter. A client who wants the booking registered under a different number can type it.

4.5 Quality and limits

Meta rates the number's quality from clients' reactions (blocks, reports). Only business-initiated template messages count against the daily limit, and the limit grows on its own from 1,000 unique clients a day after verification. The assistant sends templates only in response to something that happened on the client's own booking, which is the pattern Meta treats well.

7. Privacy and data protection

  • What is stored, and where. The client's name, WhatsApp number, the vehicle and route, the documents they send, the conversation needed to answer questions, and the audit trail of what happened — in MKY's own Postgres database and file storage in Frankfurt (EU). Nothing about a booking is stored anywhere else.
  • What Meta sees. Meta carries the messages and media between the client and the assistant, under the WhatsApp Business terms, and keeps media available to the business for a limited period. Meta does not receive the booking data the assistant derives.
  • What OpenAI sees. The text or images of documents and the client's messages, for reading and answering, under API terms that exclude training on that data. Model output is validated by code before anything is stored.
  • Who can read what. A client sees only their own bookings and shipments; a forwarded reference reveals nothing. The database refuses public access entirely; the console needs a name and the admin secret. Every action by a client or an operator is logged without document contents or secrets.
  • The privacy policy page. Meta requires one for the app; it should say the above in plain words: what is collected, why (to make and manage a shipment booking), where it is held, how long, and how to ask for it to be removed. Deleting a client's data is a matter of deleting their client row, bookings and files.
  • Retention. How long to keep conversations and documents after a shipment is delivered is a decision for MKY; the system can apply whatever period is chosen.
  • Egyptian law. Egypt's Personal Data Protection Law (No. 151 of 2020) sets duties similar to Europe's for data about Egyptian residents; storing the data in the EU and having a policy and a deletion path covers the practical requirements. Formal registration duties under the law should be confirmed with MKY's legal adviser.

9. Engineering notes: what changes in the code

For the engineers. The engine, the flows, the readers, the document pipeline, the outbox, the console and the tests are untouched. The work is one new transport and a few channel-aware seams.

9.1 New: api/whatsapp.js and lib/whatsapp.js

  • Webhook verification. GET /api/whatsapp answers Meta's hub.challenge when hub.verify_token matches WHATSAPP_VERIFY_TOKEN.
  • Inbound. POST /api/whatsapp: verify X-Hub-Signature-256 (HMAC-SHA256 of the raw body with the app secret); claim each message id in processed_updates (Meta retries when it gets no 200, exactly as Telegram does); map each message to the engine's input kinds — text, an interactive reply (button_reply.id / list_reply.id → the same callback payloads the keyboards emit today), a document or image (media id, filename, mime, caption), a shared contact; ignore statuses and reactions. Answer 200 at once; read files in the background under waitUntil, as the Telegram transport does now.
  • Outbound. sendText, sendButtons (≤ 3), sendList (≤ 10 rows), sendDocument (upload, then send by media id), sendContact (the direct line as a vCard), markRead and the typing indicator, sendTemplate (name, language, components). One fetch wrapper with the token, mirroring lib/telegram.js.
  • Keyboards → interactive messages. A small adapter turns the inline rows from lib/flow/keyboards.js into reply buttons when there are at most three, and into a list otherwise; the ids are the existing callback strings (well under WhatsApp's 256-byte limit). The reply keyboard used for "Share my number" is not needed: the client's wa_id is their number.
  • Sessions and identity. Session key whatsapp:<wa_id>; clients gains whatsapp_id (one small migration); the client is upserted from the profile name and number on every message. ctx.channel = 'whatsapp' flows into bookings, documents, tickets and tasks as today.
  • Step 1 on WhatsApp. askNextBasic offers the known number with a "Use this number" button instead of the share keyboard; typing another number works as it does now.

9.2 The 24-hour window in the outbox

  • Record last_client_message_at per session on every inbound message.
  • The drain, for a whatsapp row: inside the window → render as today; outside → send the template that corresponds to the event type, with the PDF as a document header where there is one, and a quick-reply button. Template names and languages are settings, so the desk can swap wording after re-approval without a deploy.
  • Six templates (section 4.1), each in ar and en, category utility.

9.3 Things that do not exist on WhatsApp

  • Pinned card: refreshPin is Telegram-only already and stays so; for WhatsApp, the same events that edit the card send a short status message instead (inside the window) or the update template (outside).
  • Editing sent messages / clearing buttons: not possible; the engine's refusal of stale taps covers it.
  • Commands: "menu", "start", "cancel" and their Arabic forms are already understood as text; /reset becomes "reset".

9.5 Hosting

The Hobby plan allows twelve server functions and the project uses twelve. Either fold api/status.js into api/health.js to make room, or move to Vercel Pro — recommended for production for the reasons in section 6.

9.6 Testing

The engine tests need no change. The transport gets its own tests with recorded Meta webhook payloads (text, button reply, list reply, document with caption, three documents in a burst, a contact) driven through the same in-memory database, and a signature-verification test. End-to-end checks run against Meta's free test number before the real one is verified.
