The short version

Same assistant, second door

Everything built for Telegram — the three-step booking, the document reading, tracking, the agent hand-off, the Operations console — sits behind one engine that does not care which app the client uses. WhatsApp is a second door into the same system. Both can run side by side.

What carries over

Every step, every rule, every screen at the desk, and all of the testing. The client's data stays in the same place.

What WhatsApp adds

A Meta business account and a verified business, a dedicated phone number, a 24-hour rule for messages MKY starts, buttons limited to three per message, and a small per-message price.

The long pole

Meta's business verification: usually two to ten business days. The software work runs in parallel and is not the bottleneck.

For the client

What stays the same

  • The three steps: your details → the vehicle and its papers → your booking, with "Step 1 of 3" and so on.
  • The chassis check that stops a unit being booked twice.
  • Everything in one message with the papers attached — one answer after all the files are read.
  • Documents read automatically, chassis cross-checked, a wrong paper flagged, an unclear one asked about.
  • The summary card, the booking reference and the PDF copy.
  • Track my shipment — status, vessel, arrival date from MKY's records; only your own shipments.
  • Talk to an agent, in hours and after hours, with the urgent question and the direct number.
  • Arabic and English in every message, and every safety rule: nothing invented, nothing shown that is not yours.

WhatsApp's rules

The 24-hour rule, in plain words

From the moment a client writes, MKY may reply freely for 24 hours. Every booking, every tracking request, every question happens inside that window — and it is free.

After 24 hours of silence, MKY may only send messages that Meta approved in advance — templates with blanks for the reference and the details. They are charged per message. The client tapping "Reply" reopens the window.

The assistant needs six templates, in Arabic and English:

  • Booking confirmed, with the PDF
  • More information needed
  • Booking not confirmed, with the reason
  • MRN issued
  • Shipment update
  • A message from the team
✅ Your booking MKY-BKG-260916-0AFH is confirmed. Shipment MKY-26041. Your PDF copy is attached. [ Reply ]
⚠️ We need a little more to continue your booking MKY-BKG-260916-0AFH: a clearer copy of the invoice. Reply here to send it. [ Reply ]

Templates are reviewed by Meta, usually within hours. The wording can be changed later by submitting a new version.

Setting up

Two ways to connect

Directly with Meta (recommended)

  • MKY holds the accounts and the number in its own name.
  • No middleman and no monthly fee; only Meta's per-message prices.
  • MKY does the verification itself, with our help on the technical parts.

Through a provider (Twilio, 360dialog, Infobip)

  • The provider walks MKY through the same verification and can host the number.
  • Adds a monthly fee (roughly $50–100 per number) or about half a cent per message on top of Meta's prices.
  • Slightly less setup effort; one more company in the chain.

The software is almost identical either way; the choice is about who holds the account and the extra fee.

Trust

Where the data lives, and who sees what

  • MKY's own database and file storage in Frankfurt (EU) hold the client's name and number, the vehicle and route, the documents, and the record of what happened. Nothing about a booking is kept anywhere else.
  • Meta carries the messages between the client and the assistant, encrypted, and keeps media available to MKY for a limited time. It does not receive the booking data.
  • The AI provider sees the text or image of a document while reading it, under terms that forbid training on it.
  • A client sees only their own bookings and shipments. Every action is logged, without document contents.
  • A privacy page on MKY's website says what is collected, why, where, for how long, and how to ask for it to be removed — Meta requires it, and it answers the questions clients ask.
  • Egypt's data protection law (No. 151 of 2020) is covered in practice by keeping data in the EU with a policy and a deletion path; formal registration should be confirmed with MKY's legal adviser.
